Postdoctoral Fellow · Harvard SEAS

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard University working on flat optics and structured light. My research spans the design and fabrication of high-numerical-aperture metalenses, meta-optics for optical atom traps, and spatio-temporally structured light fields. I completed my Ph.D. at the College of Optics and Photonics (CREOL), University of Central Florida, where I studied the physics and applications of space-time wave packets.

Research

My work lies at the intersection of photonics, flat optics, and structured light. I am broadly interested in controlling the spatial, temporal, and spectral degrees of freedom of light to enable new functionalities in imaging, sensing, and quantum technologies.

◆

Flat Optics & Metalenses

Design and fabrication of high-numerical-aperture (NA > 0.8) metalenses operating across the visible spectrum, with applications in metrology and precision imaging. [Placeholder — research directions being finalized]

◆

Space-Time Structured Light

Theoretical and experimental exploration of spatio-temporally structured wave packets — light fields with tightly correlated spatial and spectral degrees of freedom — and their unique propagation, refraction, and nonlinear properties. [Placeholder — research directions being finalized]

◆

Quantum & Atom Optics

Meta-optics for generating high-uniformity optical neutral atom traps with >10,000 trap sites, in collaboration with the Lukin Group (Harvard) and QuEra. [Placeholder — research directions being finalized]
