Research topic

Space-time wave packets

A pulsed beam normally spreads in two ways: diffraction widens it in space, and dispersion stretches it in time. In free space its peak also always travels at the speed of light, c. A space-time wave packet is a pulsed beam in which each wavelength is tied to one specific propagation angle. This one-to-one correlation between the spatial and temporal frequencies changes how the pulse propagates: it no longer diffracts over long distances, and its group velocity becomes a parameter that is set by the shape of the spectrum rather than by the medium.

During my Ph.D. and postdoctoral work at CREOL with Ayman Abouraddy, we used this freedom to tune the group velocity in free space above and below c, to make wave packets accelerate or decelerate along their path, to build a free-space optical delay line, and to show that these pulses obey their own law of refraction at an interface between two materials. Later work localized the wave packets in all three dimensions, gave them orbital angular momentum and topological polarization textures, and carried the concept into planar waveguides, multimode fibers, surface plasmon polaritons and on-chip lasers.

The theory, synthesis methods and experiments are collected in our review in Advances in Optics and Photonics (2022) and in a tutorial on spatiotemporal Fourier synthesis in JOSA A (2025).

Key results

  • Free-space delay line. Two wave packets about 1.3 ps wide, travelling through the same free space at different group velocities, separated by about 160 ps: a delay–bandwidth product of about 100. Nat. Commun. 11, 5782 (2020)
  • Acceleration in free space. By making the spatial bandwidth vary across the spectrum, the group velocity changes along the propagation axis, by about 1.2c over roughly 20 mm in the experiments, in both the subluminal and superluminal regimes. Phys. Rev. Lett. 125, 233901 (2020)
  • Anomalous refraction. At a planar interface the group velocity of a space-time wave packet can stay unchanged, or even increase on entering a higher-index material; a new law of refraction was tested in several optical materials. Nat. Photon. 14, 416 (2020)
  • Localized in all dimensions. A radial chirp added through conformal coordinate transformations gives propagation-invariant wave packets confined in all three dimensions, with group velocity tunable from 0.7c to 1.8c and prescribed orbital angular momentum. Nat. Commun. 13, 4573 (2022)
  • Space-time optical merons. A meron polarization texture imprinted on the spectral surface of an ultrafast pulse in momentum–energy space gives a freely propagating topological structure localized in three dimensions. Nat. Commun. 16, 8592 (2025)
  • On-chip sources. An integrated semiconductor laser array emits space-time wave packets directly, without external pulse shaping. Nat. Photon. 20, 1174 (2026)
